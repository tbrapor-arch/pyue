## Reading Jyutping

Every line in this course is written twice: in **characters** (written Cantonese, traditional script) and in
**Jyutping**, the romanisation of the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong. Jyutping writes each syllable as
initial + final + **tone number**: 廣東話 gwong2 dung1 waa2.

### The six tones
| Tone | Contour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | high level (or high falling) | 詩 si1 'poem' |
| 2 | high rising | 史 si2 'history' |
| 3 | mid level | 試 si3 'try' |
| 4 | low falling | 時 si4 'time' |
| 5 | low rising | 市 si5 'market' |
| 6 | low level | 事 si6 'matter' |

Syllables ending in **-p, -t, -k** are short and only take tones 1, 3 and 6: 識 sik1, 錫 sek3, 食 sik6.

### Letters that surprise English readers
| Jyutping | Sounds like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| j | English y | 人 jan4 |
| z, c | ts, ts' (c is aspirated) | 走 zau2, 車 ce1 |
| yu | French u | 魚 jyu4 |
| oe, eo | French eu / œ | 靴 hoe1, 去 heoi3 |
| aa / a | long a / short a | 三 saam1, 心 sam1 |
| ng- | the ng of 'singer', at the start of a syllable | 我 ngo5 |
| m, ng alone | a syllable with no vowel | 唔 m4, 五 ng5 |

### Changed tones (變音)
Some words are said with a rising tone (2, sometimes 1) instead of their dictionary tone, especially at the end of a
word or in nicknames: 女 neoi5 'woman' but 個女 go3 neoi2 'daughter'. The lessons write the tone you hear.

### In the app
Tap a word to open its card. The toolbar switches between characters + Jyutping, Jyutping on tap, Jyutping only
and characters only. Exercises accept Jyutping with tone numbers, with or without spaces (zung1ji3 = zung1 ji3).
